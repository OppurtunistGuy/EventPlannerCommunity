## Customer Experience Suggestion (Separate from UI Improvements)

The current flow appears to expose restaurant operations (table management, kitchen workflow, order status, etc.) to the customer. While these are essential for staff, they don't add value to the dining experience and can create unnecessary complexity.

### Suggested Customer-Centric Flow

The customer interface should focus only on actions that matter to the guest.

1. **Table Assignment**

   * The waiter assigns the customer to a table upon arrival.
   * This happens seamlessly in the background; the customer doesn't need to see table numbers or restaurant capacity.

2. **Order Placement**

   * The waiter takes the customer's food and drink requests and adds them to the assigned table.
   * The system handles all backend processes automatically.

3. **Hide Operational Details**

   * Do **not** display statuses such as:

     * Order sent to kitchen
     * Kitchen preparing
     * Table management
     * Restaurant operations
     * Internal workflows
   * These are operational concerns for staff, not for guests.

4. **Customer Order Review**

   * The customer should only see:

     * Current food and drink items
     * Quantity
     * Running total
     * Taxes/service charges (if applicable)
     * Ability to review the order before requesting the bill

5. **Bill & Payment**

   * Once dining is complete, the customer reviews the final bill generated for their table.
   * They can then proceed to payment through the available payment methods.

### Design Principle

The customer interface should answer only three questions:

* **What have I ordered?**
* **How much is my current bill?**
* **How do I pay?**

Everything else—table allocation, kitchen workflow, preparation status, and restaurant operations—should remain within the staff-facing system. This separation creates a cleaner, more intuitive customer experience while allowing the restaurant to manage operations independently.
